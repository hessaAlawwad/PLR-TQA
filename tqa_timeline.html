Evolution of TQA Evaluation Methods

From Early Baselines to Modern Transformer Approaches (2017-2024)

📊 Early Baselines & Graph Methods Era

2017
MemN (Kembhavi et al.)
Core Innovation: Introduced the TQA Dataset with hierarchical memory networks and IR approach for lesson-relevant paragraph selection.
Key Limitations: Lesson-constrained approach with modest baseline accuracy (34%). Did not address the "out-of-domain lesson" problem.
2018
IGMN (Li et al.)
Core Innovation: Graph-based reasoning focused on essay-level contradictions. Constructed Contradiction Entity-Relationship Graph (CERG) to inform memory networks.
Key Limitations: Complex and brittle graph construction. Limited performance gains (41% accuracy). Focused heavily on contradiction - a narrow aspect of TQA.
2019
F-GCN (Kim et al.)
Core Innovation: Multimodal context graphs using TF-IDF for initial retrieval, then constructing multimodal graphs with Graph Convolutional Networks for feature fusion.
Key Limitations: Reliance on sparse retrieval - TF-IDF was a bottleneck, unable to capture deep semantic meaning in the initial retrieval step.
2021
RAFR (Ma et al.)
Core Innovation: Fine-grained relation awareness, reasoning over nodes of a diagram graph, focusing on textual relationships within the diagram.
Key Limitations: Visually limited - primarily considered text on diagrams, potentially ignoring crucial non-textual visual information and broader lesson context.

🚀 The Pre-training Era: Transformer-Based Approaches

2020
ISAAQ (Gomez-Perez & Ortega)
Core Innovation: Transformer-based solver - one of the first to heavily leverage transformer LMs. Used multiple retrievers (IR, NSP, NN) and an ensemble of solvers.
Key Limitations: Generic retrieval - retrieval components were not jointly trained or optimized for the solver. Complex ensemble approach serving as a key performance benchmark.
2022
WSTQ (Ma et al.)
Core Innovation: Weakly supervised learning using RoBERTa for text and CNNs for diagrams. Applied weakly supervised objectives for text matching and relation detection.
Key Limitations: Focus on generator - innovations were primarily in training the generator/reader, with less focus on optimizing the initial retrieval step itself.
2023
MoCA (Xu et al.)
Core Innovation: Domain pre-training & cross-attention. Introduced domain pre-training for RoBERTa on TQA corpus and used cross-attention for fusing text and vision transformer features.
Key Limitations: High-performing baseline achieving SOTA at the time by focusing on better feature fusion. However, initial retrieval of context was still a separate, non-optimized step.
2024
MRHF (Zhu et al.)
Core Innovation: Multi-stage retrieval & hierarchical fusion. Key baseline for our work using a hybrid retrieval pipeline (sparse → dense bi-encoder → cross-encoder) and mixture-of-experts architecture.
Key Limitations: Sophisticated but generic retrieval representing SOTA in complex, non-utility-aware retrieval. Multi-stage reranking is computationally heavy and still optimized for generic relevance, not downstream utility.
